# Market research: what businesses sell and raise for (October 2026)

This is the data behind the three valuators. It was compiled in October 2026 from published reports and, where the primary report was paywalled or unreachable, from broker and advisor summaries that quote it. Those cases are marked. Check the primary report before quoting any single figure to a client.

**How the figures were checked (22 October 2026).** This build environment's network policy blocks direct downloads from the publishers' sites (bizbuysell.com, softwareequity.com, carta.com, argos.fund, stern.nyu.edu). The headline figures were therefore cross-checked with web searches restricted to each publisher's own domain. Figures marked ✓ matched the publisher's own page; the rest come from summaries and are marked as such.

All amounts are US dollars unless stated.

### Pre-revenue methods

| Method | How it works | Calibration used |
|---|---|---|
| **Scorecard** (Bill Payne) | Median pre-money of comparable funded startups × weighted comparison: team 30%, opportunity 25%, product 15%, competition 10%, sales channels 10%, need for more funding 5%, other 5%. | Comparison factor 50%–150% per item. |
| **Berkus** (Dave Berkus) | Up to $500K for each of five risk-reducing milestones: sound idea, prototype, team, strategic relationships, product rollout or sales. $2.5M ceiling. | Each factor worth up to 25% of the market baseline, which keeps Berkus's ceiling-to-market ratio (1.25x) as round sizes have grown. |
| **Risk factor summation** | Baseline adjusted across 12 risks, each scored −2 to +2, classically $250K per point. | 12.5% of baseline per point ($250K on a $2M baseline). |
| **Venture capital method** | Post-money today = exit value × (1 − later dilution) ÷ (1 + target return)^years. | Target returns by stage ✓ (Damodaran, Table 2, after Plummer and Sahlman): start-up 50–70%, first stage 40–60%, second stage 35–50%, bridge/IPO 25–35%. Damodaran notes early-stage VC actually earned ~21% a year over 20 years: the targets price in failures. |

Sources: [WaveUp methods guide](https://waveup.com/blog/startup-valuation-methods/), [MicroVentures](https://microventures.com/how-to-determine-the-pre-revenue-value-of-a-startup), [Damodaran, valuing young companies](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pdfiles/papers/younggrowth.pdf).

## 6. Assumptions we made (and where they come from)

| Assumption | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS growth curve | 0% → 2.5x, 15% → 4.0x, 30% → 5.5x, 50% → 7.5x, 80% → 10x, 120% → 13x | Fitted so the median company (15% growth) hits SEG's 4.0x and each growth band lands on the published range. |
| Scale factor | 0.5x at $100K ARR to 1.4x at $100M ARR, 1.0 at $5M | ARR-band data from multiple advisors. |
| Business model factors | AI-native 1.5, marketplace 0.75, fintech 0.8, consumer app 0.7, hardware 0.45, e-commerce 0.25, tech services 0.3 | E-commerce and tech services calibrated to BizBuySell's 1.09x and 1.12x revenue; AI sits between the SaaS floor and AI funding headlines. |
| NRR factor | ±1.5% per point from 102%, capped 0.7–1.35 | Partial version of the public NRR spread (growth is already counted). |
| VC exit multiple cap | 12x revenue | Top of the private SaaS range for 80%+ growth. |
| Value-driver adjustments (SME) | −25% to +15% each, combined −50% to +40% | Advisor rules of thumb above. |
| Size premium (SME income approach) | 6% at $250K EBITDA to 2% at $20M | Judgment; editable in `src/benchmarks.js`. |
| Currency rates | EUR 0.86, GBP 0.75, CAD 1.38, AUD 1.52, CHF 0.80, AED 3.6725, MAD 9.2 per USD | Approximate; used only for size bands and to convert startup baselines. Editable in the app. |
| Pre-seed baselines where only seed is published | seed × 0.625 | Carta's 2025 ratio of the $10M pre-seed SAFE cap to the $16M seed pre-money. Used for Europe, UK and "US outside SF and NYC". |

| UK method weight | equal to the US multiple method | US figures are deal data; UK figures are adviser ranges. Equal weight balances local relevance against data quality. |
